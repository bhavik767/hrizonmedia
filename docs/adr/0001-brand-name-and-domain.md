# Brand name and domain: encryptstream.com

We needed a name for a secure video platform competing in VdoCipher's market, on a
blog-led SEO strategy where domain authority accrues over months and a later rename
would cost accumulated rankings and backlinks. We chose **EncryptStream** on
`encryptstream.com` — descriptive, unambiguous when spoken aloud, and free of the
trademark exposure that sank the more distinctive candidates.

## Considered Options

Roughly 1,600 `.com` domains were checked against Verisign RDAP and DNS. The
rejections that matter:

- **cipher / cypher family** (`ciphervideo.com` was available) — rejected on trademark
  grounds. `VdoCipher` is a direct competitor in this exact product category, and `Vdo`
  is "video" with the vowels dropped, making their brand phonetically "Video Cipher."
  `CipherVideo` is the same two words transposed, sold to the same buyers. Prior art
  also exists: General Instrument's VideoCipher satellite scrambling system.
- **`krptostream.com`** — available, but it is a vowel-less misspelling whose correct
  spelling (`kryptostream.com`) is owned by someone else, so word-of-mouth traffic would
  leak permanently. "Krypto" also now reads as cryptocurrency, which would put our blog
  in the wrong keyword space.
- **`knox` family** — Samsung Knox is an established mobile-security brand operating in
  the same technical territory as Widevine L1.
- **Coined single words** (Latin roots, invented five-letter words) — availability is
  effectively zero at `.com`; 375 of 400 five-letter invented domains were registered.
- **Premium purchases** (`viewonce.com`, `ripstop.com`) — better names, but they carry a
  four-to-five-figure cost that is better spent on content at this stage.

## Consequences

- The name is descriptive rather than distinctive, so it is a weaker trademark and
  harder to defend against similar names. Accepted deliberately in exchange for instant
  comprehension and zero collision risk.
- Register `.io` and `.co` as defensive redirects.
- Domain availability was verified as a point-in-time snapshot. One candidate
  (`sigilos.com`) was registered by a third party mid-evaluation.
